## What the numbers say

The organic channel is flat and the demo is deliberately out of the index, so
the traffic that arrives is the traffic the repository's own front page
generates. At 262 unique visitors a fortnight, reaching 10 stars a day means
roughly 3 700 unique visitors a day at the same conversion — or a channel of a
different kind entirely.

Clones are the one number that moved: +2 050 clones and +85 unique cloners
over the window. That is what a release wave looks like from the inside —
people pulling the source to look at it or to run it. The stars it produced
were not proportional: the same window brought 2 stars.

Two referrars are worth a second look. `t.co` brought two views — the only
social referrer in the window, and the direction the distribution work in
TaskMaster #14/#15 wants to grow. `ashishpatel26.github.io` is a third-party
blog that links the repository on its own, which is the shape a directory
entry has when it works: an inbound link from a page the project did not
write.

The repository's own open-PR count fell from 14 to 0 (the Dependabot backlog
and the promotion PR were closed), and the contributor count rose from 6 to 7.

## Method

- Repository stats, traffic and referrers: the GitHub REST traffic API
  (`/traffic/views`, `/traffic/clones`, `/traffic/popular/referrers`).
- Star growth: the GraphQL stargazer edges, ordered by `STARRED_AT`, counted
  against the collection timestamp.
- Traffic window: 14 days, the same window as the previous snapshots, so the
  two are comparable.

No causal claim is made: GitHub's traffic data cannot attribute a star to a
referrer, and the referral links the launch wave will carry are not yet in
the window.
